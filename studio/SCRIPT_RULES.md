# Script rules (narrated long-form)

Written after dnz flagged a first script as "sloppy AI". Every narration script follows these, and
every draft passes the audit at the bottom before dnz sees it. These rules are about sounding human;
the channel's VOICE (tone, humour, how simple) comes from the reference study and goes in the last
section.

## Rules
- Write for the ear. If a line wouldn't come out of a person's mouth, rewrite it.
- No em dashes, no semicolons, no "…" dramatic pauses in narration.
- No "not X, it's Y", "not X. Y.", "this isn't X, this is Y", or "Not A. Not B. C." constructions.
- No filler intensifiers: actually, really, literally, basically, honestly, just, very, truly, simply.
- No fake reveal transitions: "here's the thing", "here's the part nobody tells you", "stay with me",
  "which brings us to", "now here's the question", "let that sink in", "but wait".
- No metronome: never three sentences in a row of the same length or shape. At most one sentence
  under five words per paragraph. No "Word. Word. But word." tricolons.
- At most one rhetorical question per chapter (dialogue excluded).
- Every sentence adds new information or moves the story. Delete the rest.
- Specifics over adjectives: a number, a name, a date, a place, an object. Fix a vague line by
  replacing it with a fact, not by rewording it.
- Jokes come from real, specific details, not punchline fragments.
- Hedge only where evidence is thin ("reportedly"). Mark unchecked facts [SOURCE?].
- Never claim something the comments can easily debunk.
- Avoid time-relative words ("this October", "last year"): name the year. People watch years later.
- Write hard names so the voice can say them (add a phonetic note for the VO step).

## Audit (run before sending a draft)
1. Run the tell counter (em dash, ellipsis, intensifiers, "not X" lines, reveal phrases, question
   marks, sentences under 5 words). Target zero for the banned items. Report the counts.
2. Flag any 3+ sentences in a row with the same length.
3. Flag every claim without a source in the fact-check notes.
4. Read the first 60 words as a stranger: would they keep watching? If not, rewrite the hook.
5. Word count vs target runtime at the chosen voice's measured words per minute.

## Channel voice (filled 2026-10-22 from the How-So study; dnz: copy How-So's script style)
Evidence: `references/howso-medieval/NOTES.md` (REF-A) and `references/howso-bronze-age/NOTES.md`
(REF-B), section 9, plus `data/transcript_timed.md` in each folder.
- Tone: calm, confident, third-person documentary. One story of cause and effect, told like a
  teacher who knows the ending. No hype words, no exclamations, no questions to the viewer, never
  "you". "We" only in the frame: "Our story begins...", "what we call...", "In the next video, we
  will..." (REF-A: 0 questions, 0 "you"; REF-B: 9 "we", all framing)
- How simple / how deep: simple words, real depth. Median sentence 13 words (10% are 7 or fewer,
  10% are 22 or more, max about 35). About 1.2-1.4 dates per minute. Name a term once, then use it
  ("the so-called Dark Ages", "Pax Romana"). Explain WHY things happened, not only what. Pick a
  scope and say it (REF-A follows the Carolingian heartland).
- Humour: none. Off-limits: jokes, modern slang, pop-culture references, opinions on modern politics.
- Hook pattern (17-41 s): line 1 names the era and the place ("In the ancient Near East, the
  world's first civilizations emerged."). Then the sweep of the whole video in one or two lines, as
  a chain or a list of pairs ("farming villages grew into fortified cities, cities linked to form
  kingdoms, and kingdoms expanded into vast empires"; "Nobles and kings, faith and warfare, culture
  and learning, trade and innovation"). Optional: the tension ("Yet the path was never
  straightforward.") and a roll call of peoples. Last line = the title line: "This is the story of
  how Europe was born." / "This is the Bronze Age Near East." Then 1-2 s of black. (REF-A 0:00-0:17,
  REF-B 0:00-0:41)
- Prologue: the world before, starting "Our story begins..." (REF-B 0:49) or with the old order
  (REF-A 0:19 Rome), ending on the ingredients of the era ("Romans, Christians, barbarians.").
- Chapter open: a time anchor + place + the new situation or the new player. "Around 2000 BCE, a
  new player entered the stage, the Amorites." "In the 10th century, things started to change."
  (REF-B 12:37, REF-A 9:32)
- Chapter close: one short line (4-10 words) that points forward, then a 5-6 s pause on the next
  chapter card. "Europe was under siege." "A great calamity was approaching." "The entire Near East
  was fragmenting, but not for long." (REF-A 7:58, 17:39; REF-B 15:54) This is our one bridge line
  per chapter. Do not reuse the same closer shape twice in a video.
- Inside a chapter: when two regions run in parallel, alternate them ("Meanwhile, important
  developments occurred in the land of the Nile."). (REF-B 4:33, 14:46)
- Story shape: growth, prosperity, crisis, a new player. Name the cycle in the hook and come back to
  it. (REF-B 0:19, 12:27)
- Fragments for weight are allowed, one per paragraph at most: "The first cities." "A king."
  (REF-B 2:52, 6:06)
- Epilogue (30-50 s): what the era left behind, link to today or to the next era, then a teaser for
  the next video. No "subscribe", no "like". (REF-A 21:20, REF-B 21:28-21:49)
- Length: about 2,550-2,650 words for about 22 minutes (REF-A 2,645, REF-B 2,541). Measure against
  our chosen voice before locking.
- Sentence openers to vary: "The", "In the", "This", "But", "These" are the references' top five.
  Watch that no opener repeats three times in a row.
